# Batch 4 — Ruth → United Monarchy

## Goal

Extend the atlas from the end of Judges through Ruth, Samuel, Saul, David, and Solomon while making Jerusalem/Zion the first specialized historical-geography experience in the project.

The core editorial rule remains unchanged: **textual importance does not justify false cartographic precision**.

## Canonical coverage

Batch 4 deliberately includes **Ruth** so the atlas does not leave a canonical gap between Judges and Samuel.

The principal texts represented are:

- Ruth
- 1 Samuel
- 2 Samuel
- 1 Chronicles (parallel monarchy material)
- 1 Kings 1–11
- 2 Chronicles 1–9

The app does not attempt to resolve every source-critical or chronological question inside those books. It maps their geography and clearly marks the archaeological/historical confidence of related identifications.

## Ruth

- Moab and Bethlehem reuse established earlier content-pack records.
- The Moab → Bethlehem route is drawn only as a broad reconstructed corridor.
- No specific Jordan ford, field of Boaz, threshing floor, or Bethlehem house is claimed.

## Samuel and the ark

### Shiloh

Shiloh remains an established archaeological site, but no excavated structure is labeled as the biblical tabernacle.

### Ramathaim-zophim and Ramah

- Ramathaim-zophim remains unpinned.
- Samuel’s Ramah is shown at the er-Ram area only as a **probable** identification.

### Aphek and Ebenezer

- Aphek is securely anchored at Tel Afek.
- Ebenezer is deliberately unpinned.
- The ark animation uses Aphek as the secure Philistine-camp anchor; it does not pretend that Aphek is Ebenezer.

### Ark route

The route through Ashdod → Gath → Ekron → Beth-shemesh → Kiriath-jearim is a textual city sequence. Connecting lines are schematic.

## Saul

### Gibeah

The existing Gibeah/Tell el-Ful record remains probable, not absolute.

### Michmash and Geba

Batch 4 adds the Mukhmas/Jabaʿ landscape and Wadi Suweinit ravine as a **probable topographic context** for 1 Samuel 13–14.

The named crags Bozez and Seneh are not assigned to individual modern rocks.

### Gilgal

Samuel-era Gilgal remains unpinned because the relevant references do not justify assuming one modern location.

### Jabesh-gilead

The Saul rescue episode references the earlier unlocated Jabesh-gilead record. No march path is drawn to a guessed site.

### Endor and Gilboa

- Endor is shown as an approximate regional anchor.
- Mount Gilboa is an established landscape feature.
- Beth-shean is an established city site.

The final-campaign animation is therefore strongest at the Gilboa/Beth-shean end and more schematic elsewhere.

## Jerusalem / Zion specialist model

This batch intentionally separates several concepts that are often collapsed in popular maps.

### Jerusalem

Geographically established city. The size and monumentality of the 10th-century settlement remain archaeologically debated.

### Southeastern ridge / “City of David” archaeological zone

An established topographic and archaeological area south of the Temple Mount. The modern archaeological label is not treated as the exact boundary of every biblical use of “City of David.”

### Zion

`zion-biblical` has **no coordinate**.

The term develops:

- early Davidic usage: fortress/stronghold associated with the City of David
- later usage: Jerusalem more broadly
- later temple/theological usage: Zion becomes a major sacred toponym

The atlas therefore refuses a single permanent “Zion pin.”

### Gihon Spring

An established physical anchor on Jerusalem’s eastern slope and one of the strongest mappable places in the Solomon accession narrative.

### Temple Mount / Haram al-Sharif

The physical precinct is an **established place**.

That does not make the exact First Temple footprint established.

### First Temple

The First Temple remains searchable as a first-class record but receives **no exact pin or polygon**.

The broad sacred-location tradition is strong. Direct excavation does not provide an exact footprint that can be responsibly plotted as archaeological fact.

### Araunah / Ornan threshing floor

The canonical literary link to the temple site is explicit, especially through Chronicles. No archaeological threshing-floor point is claimed.

## Solomon

### Gibeon

The city is secure. No particular excavated installation is labeled “Solomon’s high place.”

### Hazor, Megiddo, and Gezer

All three are major, securely identified archaeological sites.

The project **does not** automatically call particular six-chambered gates or monumental complexes “Solomonic.” Dating and royal attribution remain debated among archaeological chronologies.

### Tyre and Hiram

Tyre is secure. The animated Tyre → Jerusalem supply line is only a conceptual corridor because 1 Kings describes maritime timber movement and delivery logistics without providing a single traceable modern road.

### Ezion-geber and Ophir

- Ezion-geber retains the earlier Gulf of Aqaba contextual record.
- Ophir remains unlocated and unpinned.

No speculative international shipping polyline is drawn to a guessed Ophir.

### Sheba / Saba

Ancient South Arabia is the strongest broad cultural setting for Sheba. The map uses the Marib region only as an **approximate cultural anchor**.

It does not claim a palace of the Queen of Sheba, a royal itinerary, or independent archaeological proof of the visit narrated in 1 Kings 10.

## Political context polygons

Batch 4 adds generalized context regions for:

- early monarchy highland core
- Philistine pentapolis sphere
- Phoenician coast
- southern Aramean polities
- Judah–Philistia Shephelah frontier
- Jerusalem highland core
- South Arabian / Sabaean context

The “early monarchy highland core” is deliberately marked **disputed** as a political reconstruction. It is not the maximal territorial map of 1 Kings drawn as a modern border.

## Source posture

New source families include:

- *Archaeology of the Land of the Bible: 10,000–586 B.C.E.* — Amihai Mazar
- *Jerusalem in Bible and Archaeology: The First Temple Period* — Andrew G. Vaughn and Ann E. Killebrew, eds.
- *Excavating the City of David: Where Jerusalem’s History Began* — Ronny Reich
- *David and Solomon* — Israel Finkelstein and Neil Asher Silberman

Earlier atlas sources such as *The Sacred Bridge*, *Anchor Yale Bible Dictionary*, *Oxford History of the Biblical World*, *The Archaeology of Ancient Israel*, and *The Archaeology of Jordan* continue to be referenced across content-pack boundaries.

The source set intentionally includes competing archaeological perspectives rather than encoding one 10th-century chronology as consensus.
