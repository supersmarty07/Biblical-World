# Batch 5 — Divided Kingdom + Prophets

## Scope

Batch 5 extends the atlas from the end of Solomon’s reign through the divided kingdoms and the major Assyrian-era prophetic/historical horizon.

Primary canonical scope:

- 1 Kings 12–22
- 2 Kings 1–20
- relevant 2 Chronicles parallels
- Amos
- Hosea
- Jonah as literary geography plus the 2 Kings Jonah notice
- Isaiah’s eighth-century Assyrian-crisis geography
- Micah’s Samaria/Jerusalem/Shephelah geography

The batch stops before the Babylonian destruction/exile package, which belongs to Batch 6.

## Main product goals

1. Make Israel, Judah, Aram-Damascus, Phoenicia, Moab, and Assyria intelligible on a changing timeline.
2. Introduce structured historical dating without back-dating earlier biblical material with false precision.
3. Treat extra-biblical inscriptions as first-class evidence with their own source identity.
4. Distinguish secure archaeological places from uncertain biblical event spots.
5. Create strong guided stories around Elijah/Elisha, Omri/Ahab, the prophets, Samaria’s fall, Hezekiah, Lachish, and Jerusalem.

## The divided kingdoms

### Shechem and the split

Shechem is a secure ancient-city anchor for 1 Kings 12.

The conventional division date around 930 BCE is treated as **historical reconstruction**, not a date explicitly supplied by 1 Kings.

### Israel and Judah polygons

The map does not create one permanent boundary for either kingdom.

Instead, Batch 5 uses broad time-aware orientation polygons, including:

- early Northern Kingdom core
- Omride sphere
- later eighth-century Israel
- reduced Samaria-centered remnant after Tiglath-pileser III
- broad Judah orientation

These polygons are explicitly non-cadastral.

### Bethel and Dan

The narrative associates Jeroboam I with cult centers at Bethel and Dan.

- Dan/Tel Dan is a secure city/site.
- Bethel is probable in the inherited Genesis record.
- individual excavated ritual structures are not automatically assigned to Jeroboam I.

## Shoshenq I / Shishak

The biblical Shishak is commonly identified with Pharaoh Shoshenq I.

The project keeps distinct:

- biblical Jerusalem-plunder narrative;
- Egyptian evidence for a Levantine campaign;
- modern chronological/historical reconstruction.

The Karnak campaign list is **not** treated as a transcript of Kings.

## Omri and Samaria

Samaria/Sebastia is one of the strongest identifications in this period.

Batch 5 distinguishes:

- the secure capital site;
- Iron Age palace/administrative archaeology;
- the literary attribution to Omri/Ahab;
- later Assyrian use of “Samaria” as a territorial/political label.

Tirzah is modeled as probable at Tell el-Far‘ah (North), not absolute.

## Ahab and Qarqar

The Battle of Qarqar is important because it is **not** in Kings.

Shalmaneser III’s Kurkh Monolith names Ahab of Israel among a western coalition in 853 BCE.

The atlas therefore creates an event with:

- exact historical date: 853 BCE;
- no Scripture reference;
- explicit “extra-biblical context” note;
- warning that Assyrian troop totals are royal claims.

This avoids using the Bible as a placeholder citation for an event known from another source.

## Moab and the Mesha Stele

2 Kings 3 and the Mesha Stele overlap in:

- Mesha;
- Moab;
- Omride/Israelite domination;
- rebellion/conflict.

They do **not** narrate the same material in a simple one-to-one way.

The project therefore presents them as independent perspectives on a shared geopolitical world.

## Elijah

### Tishbe

Unlocated and unpinned.

### Kerith/Cherith

Unlocated and unpinned.

### Zarephath/Sarepta

Secure Phoenician archaeological city context.

### Mount Carmel

The ridge is secure.

The exact altar/event point is not.

No modern shrine is labeled “the place Elijah stood” by default.

### Jezreel and Beersheba

Secure city anchors.

The animated paths are reconstructed.

### Horeb/Sinai

The inherited Batch 3 rule remains: biblical Horeb/Sinai has no definitive coordinate.

The Elijah story does not suddenly turn Jebel Musa into an established destination.

## Elisha

2 Kings 2’s Gilgal is not automatically equated with the Joshua Gilgal point.

The guided story therefore starts its mapped sequence with better-secured Bethel/Jericho/Jordan anchors.

Naaman’s Jordan episode uses a river display anchor rather than inventing an exact bathing point.

## Jehu

Ramoth-gilead is disputed and stays unpinned.

The animated coup route starts from **broad Gilead context**, then reaches secure Jezreel and Samaria.

The Black Obelisk is treated as independent Assyrian evidence. Its label conventionally rendered as “Jehu, son/house of Omri” is explained as Assyrian dynastic/geopolitical nomenclature, not literal genealogy.

## Tel Dan Stele and Hazael

The Tel Dan fragments provide independent Aramaic royal-war context and the widely accepted “House of David” reading.

The project does not encode attribution to Hazael as certain because the authorship/precise historical reconstruction remains debated.

## Amos and Hosea

### Amos

Tekoa is a probable archaeological/town anchor.

Amos 7 places the confrontation with Amaziah at Bethel.

The text does not narrate a Tekoa → Bethel travel itinerary, so no route is fabricated.

### Hosea

The book’s Israel/Ephraim/Samaria language is mapped into the late Northern Kingdom context without assigning every oracle a year.

## Jonah

Batch 5 deliberately treats Jonah as **literary geography** without using the map to settle genre/historicity debates.

- Joppa → established
- Mediterranean → established physical context
- Tarshish → disputed/unpinned
- Nineveh → established

The animation goes from Joppa into the Mediterranean and stops. It does not choose Tartessos/Spain or another Tarshish proposal.

## Assyrian expansion

### Tiglath-pileser III

Late-eighth-century western campaigns are historically anchored and visualized with broad imperial context.

### Fall of Samaria

The atlas preserves the problem rather than choosing a slogan:

- Kings names Shalmaneser V in the siege sequence.
- Sargon II later claims conquest/deportation connected with Samaria.
- the fall of the Northern Kingdom is secure;
- final-capture attribution is historically debated.

### Deportation geography

- Gozan/Guzana/Tell Halaf → established
- Habor/Khabur → established river region
- Halah → uncertain/unpinned
- Median cities → unnamed; broad Media anchor only

The Samaria → Gozan line is a generalized deportation corridor, not an individual itinerary.

## Isaiah, Ahaz, and Hezekiah

### Upper Pool / Fuller’s Field

This is an important Jerusalem textual location in Isaiah 7 and Isaiah 36 / 2 Kings 18.

Its exact archaeological coordinate is unresolved, so it remains unpinned.

### Siloam Tunnel

The tunnel is an established physical feature.

The Siloam Inscription describes crews meeting underground.

The inscription itself does **not** name Hezekiah.

The project therefore separates:

- tunnel archaeology;
- inscription;
- biblical waterworks attribution;
- historical dating interpretation.

## Lachish and 701 BCE

Lachish is one of the strongest evidence intersections currently in the atlas:

- secure site identification;
- siege ramp;
- violent destruction archaeology;
- Sennacherib’s royal inscriptions;
- Lachish palace reliefs;
- biblical campaign references.

The event receives exact historical dating: **701 BCE**.

## Jerusalem and 701 BCE

The project distinguishes what the source streams say.

Assyrian royal inscriptions:

- claim devastation of Judah;
- describe Hezekiah confined in Jerusalem;
- claim tribute;
- do not claim capture of Jerusalem.

Biblical texts:

- narrate Assyrian threats;
- narrate Jerusalem’s deliverance;
- include theological/miraculous claims.

The atlas treats the campaign and Jerusalem’s non-capture as historical context while not converting theological claims into archaeological statements.

## Political-region methodology

Batch 5 context polygons are intentionally:

- broad;
- time-bounded;
- semi-transparent;
- dashed;
- labeled probable/disputed rather than established geometry.

They should be understood as **orientation zones**, not national borders.

## Source posture

Batch 5 adds source families for:

- divided-monarchy histories;
- Kings commentary/history;
- Northern Kingdom archaeology;
- Samaria archaeology;
- Lachish excavation;
- Neo-Assyrian royal inscriptions;
- Mesha Stele;
- Tel Dan Stele;
- Kurkh Monolith;
- Black Obelisk;
- Sargon II Samaria claims;
- Sennacherib’s 701 campaign;
- Siloam Inscription.

No modern copyrighted photograph, atlas map, excavation plate, or full copyrighted scholarly translation is bundled simply because the source is cited.
