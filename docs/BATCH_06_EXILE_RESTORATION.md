## Evidence streams

The release deliberately keeps the following source classes separate:

1. biblical/canonical narrative and prophetic texts;
2. archaeological site identification;
3. Babylonian chronicles and administrative tablets;
4. Assyrian royal inscriptions;
5. Achaemenid royal inscriptions;
6. Judean epigraphy;
7. Elephantine papyri;
8. modern historical/archaeological synthesis;
9. project-created route and border reconstructions.

One stream may illuminate another without being treated as a direct quotation or automatic confirmation.

## Critical distinctions enforced

### 597 BCE is not 587/586 BCE

The first capture/deportation under Jehoiachin and the later destruction under Zedekiah are separate events, stories, and date records.

### Cyrus Cylinder is not Ezra’s decree

The Cyrus Cylinder is used as evidence for Cyrus’s Babylonian restoration ideology and policy context. It does not name Jerusalem, Judah, Judeans, or the Jerusalem temple.

### Babylon 539 is not “proof of Daniel 5”

The fall of Babylon is independently historical. Daniel 5 is presented as a biblical/literary narrative set against that transition.

Belshazzar is historically attested. “Darius the Mede” remains historically unresolved.

### Susa is historical; Esther’s plot is not claimed as externally documented

Achaemenid Susa and palace architecture are secure. No excavated room is labeled “Esther’s banquet hall,” and no surviving Persian archive is claimed to verify the individual plot sequence.

### Kebar is Babylonian context

The Kebar/Chebar is represented in central Babylonia as an approximate canal-area anchor. It is not automatically identified with the Khabur River in northern Mesopotamia.

### Unlocated places stay unlocated

No normal map pin is shown for:

- Topheth;
- Jeremiah 13’s debated Perath;
- Egyptian Migdol in Jeremiah;
- Tel-abib;
- Al-Yahudu;
- Plain of Dura;
- Ahava;
- Casiphia;
- Teman;
- exact early Second Temple footprint;
- complete Nehemiah wall circuit.

## Late prophetic coverage

Batch 6 closes the canonical gap between Hezekiah and Josiah/restoration by adding:

- Manasseh and independent Assyrian context;
- Amon;
- Zephaniah;
- Nahum;
- Habakkuk;
- Lamentations;
- Obadiah;
- Malachi.

Nineveh’s 612 BCE fall is modeled as independent history, but that date is not used to force a single composition date for Nahum.

Obadiah’s date remains disputed.

Malachi is placed broadly in a functioning Persian-period temple context without assigning an exact regnal year or insisting that “Malachi” must be a recoverable personal name.

## Journeys

Batch 6 animations include:

- Josiah: Jerusalem → Megiddo;
- Jehoiachin: Jerusalem → Babylon;
- Zedekiah: Jerusalem → Jericho plains → Riblah;
- Jeremiah: Anathoth → Jerusalem;
- refugees: Mizpah → Tahpanhes;
- early return: Babylonia → Jerusalem;
- Ezra: Babylonia → Jerusalem;
- Nehemiah: Susa → Jerusalem;
- Cyrus/Persian transition: Iranian royal heartland → Babylon as schematic imperial context.

Every line is explicitly marked reconstructed unless only the named endpoint sequence is being asserted.

The long Mesopotamia–Levant journeys use a northern arc to avoid drawing an implausible straight line across desert. This does not mean the exact ancient road has been recovered.

## Current research limitations

Live web access was unavailable during implementation. The pack uses standard scholarly works and primary-source traditions known to the project, but release-quality academic publication should still add/check:

- page-level references;
- museum/object catalogue numbers;
- current official URLs;
- exact edition details where incomplete;
- current rights/license wording for any future image/facsimile imports;
- external gazetteer IDs such as Pleiades/Wikidata where appropriate.

The current release is designed to be historically responsible and auditable, not to claim peer-reviewed critical-GIS status.
