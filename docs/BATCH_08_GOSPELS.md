# Batch 8 — Jesus and the Gospels

## Purpose

Batch 8 extends the static, frontend-only atlas from the Herodian transition into the Gospel world. Its purpose is not to turn every Gospel place into a precise modern pin. Instead, it models four different things separately when the evidence requires it:

1. the place or movement named by the canonical text;
2. an archaeological site that can be located independently;
3. a later traditional identification;
4. a modern scholarly candidate or reconstruction.

This is especially important for Cana, Bethsaida, Bethany beyond the Jordan, Sychar, the Gerasene/Gadarene episode, the Transfiguration mountain, Pilate's praetorium, Golgotha, the burial place, and Emmaus.

## Infancy chronology

Matthew and Luke are not collapsed into a single synthetic travel itinerary.

The application keeps separate route visualizations for:

- Matthew's Bethlehem → Egypt → Nazareth narrative sequence;
- Luke's Nazareth → Bethlehem → Jerusalem → Nazareth narrative sequence.

The birth event is deliberately approximate. Matthew places the birth before the death of Herod the Great, while Luke 2 names Quirinius. Josephus places the historically attested Quirinian census of Judea after Archelaus's deposition in 6 CE. The project records the 6 CE census as a separate historical event rather than relocating it into Herod's reign or claiming to solve the chronological problem.

## John the Baptist

The Jordan baptism setting and Machaerus are treated separately.

`bethany-beyond-jordan` is an unpinned textual place. `al-maghtas` is a mapped traditional and archaeological baptism landscape. Late-antique churches and baptismal installations establish ancient Christian commemoration, not the exact point of Jesus' baptism.

The Gospels narrate John's imprisonment and execution under Herod Antipas but do not name Machaerus. The Machaerus association comes from Josephus. The event therefore cites the Gospel texts and Josephus independently.

## Cana

`cana-galilee` has no ordinary map pin.

The atlas shows separately:

- `khirbet-qana` — possible candidate;
- `kafr-kanna` — traditional pilgrimage identification.

Animated routes that use Khirbet Qana state that the candidate is being used as a visualization hypothesis rather than established Gospel geography.

## Bethsaida

The Gospel place `bethsaida-gospels` remains unpinned.

Two separate archaeological candidates are mapped:

- `el-araj-bethsaida`;
- `et-tell-bethsaida`.

Neither is labeled the established Gospel site. Shoreline change and the identification of Bethsaida/Julias remain part of the scholarly debate.

## Sea of Galilee and eastern-shore geography

Capernaum, Magdala, Tiberias, Chorazin, the Sea of Galilee, and other strong physical anchors are mapped normally.

The Gerasene/Gadarene/Gergesene textual problem is not resolved by a single pin. The atlas separately shows Gadara/Umm Qais and the later Kursi tradition while keeping the textual regional record unpinned.

## Samaria and Sychar

The app now distinguishes the **region of Samaria** from the **city of Samaria/Sebaste**.

`samaria-region-gospels` is only a regional display anchor. It prevents John 4's phrase "through Samaria" from being visually misread as a visit to the city of Samaria.

`sychar` remains unpinned. `askar-sychar-candidate` and the Jacob's Well tradition are separate records.

## Caesarea Philippi

The earlier `panium` record is reused and extended into the Gospel period with "Caesarea Philippi" as an alias. This avoids inventing a duplicate site while preserving the major first-century renaming/context.

The surrounding travel line is generalized; the Gospels do not preserve a recoverable road itinerary.

## Transfiguration

`mount-transfiguration` remains unpinned.

Mount Tabor is preserved as a traditional identification and Mount Hermon as a possible geographic proposal. The application does not claim that either identification is demonstrated by the canonical text.

Matthew 28's unnamed Galilean mountain is a **different record**: `galilee-resurrection-mountain`. It is intentionally not equated with the Transfiguration mountain, Mount Tabor, or another modern summit.

Mapped archaeology and textual event geography remain separate.

## Pilate's praetorium and trial geography

`praetorium-pilate` has no fixed coordinate.

The atlas keeps two major interpretive traditions visible:

- Herod's western palace / Citadel area — an important historical candidate for the governor's Jerusalem headquarters;
- Antonia / later Via Dolorosa tradition — historically important in Christian pilgrimage but not treated as a recovered street-by-street first-century route.

No animated Via Dolorosa route is presented as archaeologically recovered.

Pontius Pilate's prefecture remains c. 26–36 CE and is independently anchored by the Caesarea inscription as well as literary sources.

## Caiaphas

Caiaphas's high-priestly setting is modeled approximately c. 18–36 CE.

The ossuary often associated with Joseph Caiaphas is treated as supportive family-context evidence, not conclusive proof that a particular ossuary can be identified with the Gospel high priest beyond dispute.

## Golgotha and burial

The textual records `golgotha` and `tomb-jesus` remain unpinned.

Separate traditional/candidate records include:

- `holy-sepulchre`;
- `garden-tomb`.

The Church of the Holy Sepulchre tradition is presented as historically early and topographically/archaeologically compatible with a first-century extramural burial/crucifixion setting, but the app does not label the exact spot archaeologically proven.

The Garden Tomb is displayed as a later Christian tradition and preserves the archaeological dating objection that the tomb complex is generally assigned to an earlier, Iron Age horizon rather than a newly cut first-century tomb.

## Emmaus

Luke's Emmaus remains unpinned because the textual distance tradition and competing site identifications do not support one uncontested modern coordinate.

The app can display candidates/traditions such as Emmaus-Nicopolis and Qubeibeh without drawing an exact journey to `emmaus-luke`.

## Resurrection geography

The application maps the Gospel narratives and geographic traditions without encoding a verdict about supernatural claims.

`event-resurrection-tomb` and `event-galilee-appearances` are **not** classified as "symbolic." Their historical notes instead state that archaeological methods can assess places, tomb types, city topography, texts, and traditions, but cannot themselves adjudicate the supernatural claim of resurrection.

This is a methodological boundary rather than a theological conclusion.

## Political and cultural context

Ten broad regions orient the first-century map:

- Archelaus's ethnarchy;
- Antipas's Galilee;
- Antipas's Perea;
- Philip's tetrarchy;
- Roman Judaea after 6 CE;
- Roman Syria;
- Decapolis city-zone orientation;
- Nabataean context under Aretas IV;
- broad Galilean cultural orientation;
- broad Samaritan cultural orientation.

These are translucent context polygons, not surveyed borders. All remain `possible` or `probable`, never `established` geometry.

## Timeline

The timeline now extends to 40 CE and distinguishes:

- Herodian kingdom;
- Herodian succession / Archelaus transition;
- Roman Judaea with Antipas and Philip's tetrarchies;
- Pilate–Caiaphas Gospel-ministry context;
- early post-ministry Roman context.

The improved date resolution does not imply precise dates for every Gospel episode.

## Research status

This remains an evidence-aware production atlas, not a peer-reviewed critical historical GIS edition.

Live web access was unavailable during Batch 8 implementation. The project therefore preserves source/provenance records and conservative uncertainty labels, but current official URLs, current publication metadata, page-level citations, excavation report details, museum object IDs, and external gazetteer IDs should be independently verified before a release is described as academically critical or definitive.
