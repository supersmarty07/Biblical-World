# Batch 9 — Acts and Paul

## Purpose

Batch 9 extends the static atlas from the Gospel period into Acts and the Pauline mission world. The release covers Jerusalem, Judea/Samaria, Syria/Cilicia, Cyprus, Anatolia, Macedonia, Achaia, Roman Asia, the eastern Mediterranean voyage, Malta, Italy, and Rome.

The methodological priority is the same as earlier batches: a textual itinerary is not automatically a recoverable road, a later traditional site is not automatically the event site, and an independently attested person/date is not automatically independent proof of every scene in Acts.

## Canonical scope

The pack covers major geography in Acts 1–28 and selected Pauline-letter geography that materially extends the map:

- Mount of Olives, Jerusalem, Pentecost, Temple activity, Stephen;
- Philip in Samaria and the Gaza-road/Azotus/Caesarea sequence;
- Saul/Paul and Damascus, plus Paul's separate Galatians 1 reference to “Arabia”;
- Joppa, Cornelius, Caesarea, Agrippa I;
- Antioch, famine-relief context, and the Jerusalem meeting;
- Paul's first, second, and third missionary journeys;
- Cyprus, Pamphylia, Pisidia/Lycaonia, Troas, Macedonia, Athens, Corinth, Ephesus, and the Aegean coast;
- Paul's Jerusalem arrest, transfer to Caesarea, Felix/Festus/Agrippa II hearings;
- Acts 27–28 voyage, Malta, Puteoli, Via Appia approaches, and Rome;
- Pauline recipient geography in Rome, Corinth, Galatia, Macedonia, and the Lycus Valley.

The pack stores references and project-authored summaries; it does not bundle a complete Bible translation.

## Jerusalem and the earliest Acts geography

Batch 9 reuses the established Jerusalem, Temple Mount, Mount of Olives, upper-room, and Antonia records rather than duplicating them. It adds textual micro-locations where useful, but leaves them unpinned when the evidence does not support a precise point:

- the “Beautiful Gate”;
- Solomon's Portico as a broad Temple-complex feature rather than a surviving pinpoint;
- Akeldama;
- the location of Stephen's stoning.

The ascension event maps the named Mount of Olives landscape without claiming archaeology can identify the exact spot or adjudicate the supernatural claim.

## Philip and Samaria

Acts 8 is deliberately not forced into the city of Samaria/Sebaste. The event uses the broad Samaria-region record because the textual phrasing does not warrant turning the episode into a guaranteed visit to Sebaste.

The Gaza-road encounter is also not assigned to one modern highway segment. The animated line is explicitly schematic. Azotus and Caesarea are strong city anchors; the intermediate route is not a recovered itinerary.

## Paul, Damascus, and “Arabia”

Damascus is an established city and the Roman east–west street axis provides context for “Straight Street,” but the house of Judas is unpinned.

Paul's own Galatians 1 reference to “Arabia” is kept distinct from Acts. `arabia-galatians` has no exact coordinate and no animated route. The project does not use Petra as though Paul named it as his destination.

## First missionary journey and Derbe

The first journey follows the textual city sequence through Antioch, Seleucia Pieria, Cyprus, Pamphylia, Pisidian Antioch, Iconium, Lystra, and Derbe, and now preserves the narrated return through Lystra, Iconium, Pisidian Antioch, Perga, Attalia, and back to Antioch rather than compressing the return into a false direct hop.

Derbe is the key uncertainty:

- `derbe-acts` remains unpinned and `disputed`;
- `kerti-hoyuk-derbe` is a separate `candidate-site`;
- the animated journey may use Kerti Höyük only as a visualization hypothesis, with an explicit candidate warning.

Lystra is mapped as a probable/candidate identification rather than as an absolute archaeological certainty.

## Acts 15 and Galatians 2

The Jerusalem Council event cross-references Acts 15 and Galatians 2 but explicitly avoids declaring that the two texts necessarily describe the same Jerusalem visit. The relationship remains a chronological/source-critical question.

## Macedonia and Achaia

Batch 9 adds Troas, Samothrace, Neapolis, Philippi, Amphipolis, Apollonia, Thessalonica, Berea, Athens, Corinth, and Cenchreae.

At Philippi, the city is secure but the riverside prayer place and prison building are not pinned as recovered sites.

At Athens, the physical Areopagus hill is mapped, while the event note preserves the semantic issue that “Areopagus” may refer to the council/body as well as the hill.

The second journey continues beyond Ephesus to Caesarea and Antioch. Acts 18:22 says Paul “went up and greeted the church” without naming Jerusalem in the verse, so Jerusalem is not forced onto that route.

The third journey uses broad Galatia/Phrygia, Macedonia, and Achaia anchors where Acts names regions without listing every city. This avoids visually inventing a direct Ephesus-to-Troas shortcut through an otherwise unrepresented Macedonia/Greece circuit.

## Gallio as the strongest external chronology anchor

The Delphi inscription mentioning Gallio is modeled as inscriptional evidence for the proconsulship of Achaia around 51–52 CE. This is one of the strongest external anchors for Pauline chronology.

The app explicitly states that the inscription **does not mention Paul**.

The Corinth bema is an established archaeological monument, but its identification as the precise place of Paul's hearing is only a possible historical correlation.

## Claudius, famine, and chronology

The Claudian famine and the expulsion/disturbance notice connected with Aquila and Priscilla remain approximate historical correlations.

- famine context: broadly mid-40s CE, commonly c. 46–48;
- Claudian expulsion: commonly c. 49 CE;
- Gallio: c. 51–52 CE, externally anchored;
- Festus/Agrippa hearing: commonly c. 59–60 CE;
- voyage and arrival at Rome: conventional range c. 59–61 CE;
- two-year Roman house arrest: commonly c. 60–62 CE.

Only the strongest externally anchored events use `historical` dating; missionary-journey ranges remain `conventional` or `approximate`.

## Ephesus

Ephesus is one of the strongest archaeological anchors in Acts. The pack separates:

- the established city;
- the Great Theater;
- the Artemision;
- the unlocated Hall of Tyrannus.

The theater and sanctuary are not treated as proof of every narrative detail in Acts 19, and the Hall of Tyrannus is deliberately unpinned.

## Jerusalem arrest and Caesarea hearings

The Temple Mount, Antonia, Caesarea, and Antipatris provide strong geographic anchors. Exact custody rooms, audience halls, and arrest pathways are not presented as archaeologically recovered.

Felix, Festus, Agrippa II, Berenice, and Ananias are represented with Josephus as separate historical-literary context rather than as one merged narrative source.

## Acts 27–28 and Malta

The voyage is one of the most detailed travel narratives in Acts, but the line remains schematic because ancient sailing depended on winds, currents, tacking, and storm drift.

The project distinguishes:

- Malta/Melite as the mainstream island identification;
- a **specific shipwreck bay** as unknown;
- St Paul's Bay as a later/traditional hypothesis rather than a proven wreck site;
- Fair Havens as probable;
- Phoenix/Phoenice as disputed and unpinned.

No journey segment routes to a fabricated exact wreck coordinate.

## Rome and Paul's later fate

Acts ends with Paul alive under house arrest in Rome for two years. `paul-lodging-rome` is unpinned because Acts supplies no recoverable address.

Batch 9 deliberately does **not** create an exact death/martyrdom event or location for Paul. Later Christian traditions may be added in a future reception layer, but they are not silently inserted into the Acts narrative.

## Pauline-letter geography

A dedicated guided story maps recipient geography without claiming that every letter has an uncontested authorship, composition date, or composition site.

The Lycus Valley records include Colossae, Laodicea, and Hierapolis. Acts does not narrate Paul visiting Colossae, and the app does not invent such a journey.

## Research status

Live web search was unavailable during Batch 9 implementation. The pack therefore uses conservative project-authored summaries, canonical references, well-established ancient-source metadata, and uncertainty labels. Current official editions, exact inscription catalog numbers, page/section citations, excavation publication metadata, source URLs, licenses, and external gazetteer IDs should be independently rechecked before an academically critical release.
